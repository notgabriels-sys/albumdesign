Is your master the right loudness?

Drop a bounce and see its integrated LUFS and true peak, measured the way the platforms measure it (ITU-R BS.1770), then how Spotify, Apple Music and the rest are likely to turn it up or down.

How each platform will play it

PlatformReferenceGain appliedPlays at

Streaming services normalise loudness: a master louder than their reference is turned down, so extra loudness buys nothing but lost dynamics. Clubs and DJ media are not normalised, so a separate louder master makes sense there. Apple's Sound Check and YouTube are both reported to turn tracks down only, never up, which is why they are shown as never boosted below. That behaviour is observed rather than published, the same as the figures marked *.

Spotify is the only one of these we could confirm publishes a number in its own artist documentation. Figures marked * are measured consistently by third parties but are not documented by the platform, so treat them as observed rather than spec. Spotify's guidance is to keep true peak below -1 dBTP, and below -2 dBTP if your master is louder than -14 LUFS. This tool flags anything above that ceiling, so a master sitting exactly on -1.0 dBTP is not flagged; the release checklist states the same rule as "at or below".

Spotify does say to target -14 LUFS. The reason to ignore that as a mastering goal is not that they never asked, it is that normalisation makes hitting it pointless: everything louder is turned down to the same place anyway, so chasing the number costs dynamics and buys nothing back.

Measurement is ITU-R BS.1770 (K-weighting, absolute and relative gating), checked against the EBU Tech 3341 test signals. On a stereo 1 kHz sine at -23.0 dBFS this tool computes -23.01 LUFS, inside the ±0.1 tolerance. Use it to make decisions, and confirm on your own metering before you deliver.
